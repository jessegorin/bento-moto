$57.52 revenue the problem this report solves

Over 23 days the campaign spent $106.39 and brought 599 people to your website at just under 18¢ each. Those visitors took 1,042 follow-up actions — pulling up your menu, requesting directions, tapping to call. The interest is genuinely there.

But only two online orders were tracked back to those ads, worth $57.52. Against $106.39 of spend that is a return of 0.54× — roughly 54¢ of revenue for every dollar spent. That gap between strong interest and almost no orders is not a demand problem. It is a setup problem, and we found five specific causes.

The single biggest one: every ad click landed on your homepage rather than your online ordering page. Someone searching “sushi near me” at 6pm arrived at your front page and had to go looking for the order button. That alone is enough to explain most of the missing orders.

Where the money went

Roughly a quarter of the budget was never going to return an order

We reviewed the 45 highest-cost searches that triggered your ads, which together account for $59.10 — 56% of total spend. Grouping them shows the pattern clearly. The remaining $47.29 sits in a long tail of individually tiny searches that we have not categorised here.

One search term is worth singling out. A single click from someone searching “osaka coon rapids” — a different restaurant, 30 miles away — cost $4.15. That is 23 times your average click price, spent on a person who was looking for somebody else entirely.

Timing

A lot of the spend happened while your doors were shut

Bento Moto serves 11am–8pm Monday to Friday, 3pm–8pm Saturday, and is closed Sunday. The campaign had no schedule at all, so it kept bidding through every closed hour of the week.

Nine in ten of your clicks come from a phone — 557 of 599. Mobile searchers clicked at 4.97% versus 3.81% on desktop. That is exactly the behaviour you want for a neighbourhood takeout restaurant: people searching while they are out and hungry. It also means the page they land on has to work on a phone, which is another reason the ordering page beats the homepage.

Work completed

What we changed on August 11

All of the following was applied while the campaign is paused, so none of it has spent anything yet. Each change was verified in the account after it was made.

Ads repointed to your ordering page
The ad now links straight to bentomoto.com/store/23393 instead of the homepage, so a click lands on the menu with an order button. Google required this to be published as a new version of the ad, which is normal and keeps all the existing headlines and wording.
20 search terms blocked
Rival restaurants (Osaka, Tokyo Sushi, Oishii, Bamboo Kitchen, Fresh Wok), dishes you don't serve (hibachi, buffet, all-you-can-eat, Chinese, Thai, pizza, dango), towns outside your radius (Minneapolis, Coon Rapids, Forest Lake, Minnetonka) and non-customer searches (jobs, hiring, careers, recipes).
Ad schedule set to your trading hours
Ads now run 11am–8pm Monday to Friday and 3pm–8pm Saturday, and do not run at all on Sunday. This is the change that reclaims the $30.50 that had been going to closed hours.
Display network switched off
The campaign now runs on Google search results only. Banner placements were costing 43¢ a click and delivering nothing measurable.
Location targeting tightened to “presence”
Previously the 8-mile radius also included people merely interested in the area — Google's default, and the reason Minneapolis and St Paul searches were getting through. Ads now show only to people actually in your area.

Before relaunch

What we recommend next

The campaign is still paused, and turning it back on is your call. These are the remaining items, in the order they will matter most.

  1. Rebuild the keyword list around terms that actually work

    All 40 keywords are still on broad match. The fix is to keep the handful that earned engagement — “japanese food near me”, “sushi restaurant oakdale”, “japanese restaurant near me” — as tighter phrase and exact matches, and retire the vague ones like plain “sushi”. This is a rebuild rather than a toggle, so we have left it for your sign-off rather than doing it unilaterally.

  2. Replace two of the three ad links

    Your ad currently offers “Order Now”, “Our Story” and “Press”. The last two are interesting but they do not sell dinner. Swapping them for “Lunch Menu”, “Bento Boxes” or “Ramen” would give hungry searchers three ways into an order instead of one.

  3. Add the ad extras that are currently missing

    The account has no callouts and no menu highlights attached — the short phrases and dish lists that make an ad physically larger on the results page and push competitors further down. Adding these costs nothing and typically lifts click-through.

  4. Then, and only then, look at budget

    Your ads appeared for only 12% of relevant local searches. Budget explains 32% of what you missed, but weak ad relevance explains 56% — so raising the $5 daily budget before fixing relevance would mostly buy more of the same unqualified clicks. Fix quality first, then spend more.

  5. Re-check order tracking a week after relaunch

    Because the landing page has changed, we want to confirm orders are being attributed correctly before drawing conclusions from the new numbers. Two orders is too small a sample to trust either way.
